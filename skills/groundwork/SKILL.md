# Groundwork

Turn the request into a problem someone can make a decision about. Preserve the user's actual objective, even when their proposed implementation needs examination.

## Choose the interaction

For project work, inspect the supplied context and accessible evidence before asking for facts already available. Ask the few questions whose answers would change the objective, constraints, or next step. Continue useful work around unanswered questions, marking any temporary assumptions.

For an explicit mock interview, let the user lead the decomposition. Ask one question at a time, do not volunteer the complete solution, and distinguish fictional scenario details you introduce from information the user supplied. Respect a requested time budget. At the end, assess framing, prioritization, assumptions, and clarity with examples from their answers. Give a worked answer only when requested.

## Frame the decision

1. Identify the requester, people doing the work, people affected, and decision owner. Their incentives and definitions of success may differ.
2. Translate the request into an outcome. Separate the symptom, its suspected cause, and the proposed solution. Treat a suspected cause as a hypothesis until evidence supports it.
3. Establish the unit of work and workflow boundaries. For example, onboarding time might mean elapsed time from contract signature to activation, rather than reviewer handling time.
4. Identify constraints that could eliminate approaches: data access, permissions, integration ownership, budget, deadline, operating capacity, and required human decisions. Include only constraints relevant to this problem.
5. Define a primary outcome metric and a balancing metric. Specify population, measurement window, baseline status, and owner. If no baseline exists, propose how to collect it; do not invent one.
6. Rank unknowns by how much they could change the decision. State the next evidence-gathering action and what each likely answer would change.

Avoid turning discovery into a long questionnaire. When enough is known to choose a next action, produce the brief and expose remaining uncertainty.

## Output

Use a compact brief with:

- **Decision and objective:** what needs to be decided, for whom, and why.
- **Current workflow:** start/end points, actors, and unit of work.
- **Evidence and assumptions:** source-backed observations separated from hypotheses.
- **Success:** primary metric, balancing metric, baseline or baseline collection plan.
- **Constraints and boundaries:** requirements and what is outside the assignment.
- **Open questions:** prioritized unknowns, owners if known, and consequences.
- **Next action:** the smallest investigation or decision that advances the work.

Cite supplied documents, code locations, or observations when they support a claim. Do not claim stakeholder agreement that has not occurred. If asked to save the brief, use the existing project conventions and a user-authorized destination.
